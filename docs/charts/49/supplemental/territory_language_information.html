The main goal for CLDR language data is to provide approximate figures for the literate, functional population for each language in each territory: that is, the population that is able to read and write each language, and is comfortable enough to use it with computers.

Language population estimates come from various sources. In general, they are meant to represent the number of people that speak the language in they given country. When possible, the numbers include usage as both people's first language (L1) or a later language (L2, L3...) they could use in conversation or technology. Not all numbers may be up-to-date or completely comparable, as the data is collected from various sources. Much of the data is sourced by per-country census data or Ethnologue.

Literacy figures are taken from various sources, including the WorldBank, United Nations Statistics Division, and country censuses. In the absence of literacy rate figures for the specific language, the country literacy rate is used for the "Literacy %" column. The "Written %" column is an estimate of the use of the written form of the language. The literacy rate may be discounted to reflect the actual usage of the written form in normal daily life. Thus languages that are typically not written, such as Swiss German, will be given a low rate, even though the whole population could write in Swiss German.

Official status may have a few different values. "Official" means its the language of national government transactions and is recognized as that in the law -- whereas "de facto official" means it is used in practice but not written in the national law. "Official regional" is official in a subdivision of the territory. "Recognized" means that while its not the primary language, it is recognized by the government in law -- there may be missing values for recognized languages in this table.

The percentages may add up to more than 100% due to multilingual populations, or may be less than 100% due to illiteracy or because the data has not yet been gathered or processed. Languages with a small population may be omitted.
